The regime, in one line

The economy is running hot, and the bond market has taken over. S&P Global's services PMI reached 58.7 and manufacturing 56.7, both multi-year highs, with input and output price inflation at multi-year highs too. Governor Barr said "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed" and named AI investment demand among the forces pushing prices up. The 10-year added 15bp in a day; the 5-year crossed 5% for the first time since 2007; October hike odds jumped to 70%. Rates, not oil, now set the tone for every asset on this page.

What this edition gets wrong about the last one
  • The 21 Sep thesis was wrong. It said the Fed was tightening into a slowdown and that policy error was the live risk. Wednesday's surveys show the opposite: activity and price pressure at multi-year highs. The Fed is chasing strength, not crushing weakness.
  • VTIP moves from #3 to #1. A 10-year breaking out to a 19-year high is precisely the scenario short duration exists for.
  • VEA drops from #1 to #3. The dollar index reached 100.6, its highest since late July, and a stronger dollar works directly against unhedged international funds.
  • XLE stays out, deliberately — explained below.
  • Both earlier editions broke their own ranking rule. Each claimed to rank by summed conviction, yet each had one pair out of order — RSP above ITA on 19 Sep, VTIP above XLV on 21 Sep. The order was partly set by hand. This edition's order is computed from the scores and checked automatically.

Three readings of the same tape

This week the three lenses converged for the first time: trend turned, sentiment stayed fearful, and volatility moved decisively into the bond market.

01Market trend — a record, then a rate shock

The Nasdaq closed at a record on Tuesday while the S&P finished flat at 7,764.64. Wednesday reversed it: the S&P fell 0.75% to 7,706.03, the Nasdaq 1.13% to 26,936.04 and the Dow 0.68% to 51,511.59. Ten of eleven sectors fell, led by communication services, utilities and consumer discretionary — the rate-sensitive end of the market. Through 18 September the S&P was up 11.7% on price for the year.

Participation is still narrow — roughly 33% of members above their 50-day average, the Magnificent Seven at 33.9% of index weight. Equal weight is ahead for the year but its lead is shrinking: RSP was up about 16% against 13% for the S&P in late August, and September's AI rally has cut that to roughly a point.

02Broad sentiment — still fearful, slightly less so

CNN's Fear & Greed Index read 35 on 23 September, up from 29 on the 21st but still in "Fear". It has not reached neutral once during a month in which the Nasdaq set records. One tracker reported 71 for the same day; that figure conflicts with three others and is disregarded here.

Institutional positioning agrees with the mood. The S&P Global Investment Manager Index fell 19 points for September, and gold ETFs took in $18bn in August — the second-largest month on record — lifting global gold ETF assets 16% to $615bn.

03Volatility — calm in stocks, breaking in bonds

Equity volatility barely registered the move: the VIX closed at 14.21 on Wednesday, up 6.83%. That implies Tuesday's close near 13.3 — which means the 14.81 shown in the 21 September edition was a stale quote, as that edition cautioned. The bond market is where the stress is: the 10-year rose about 15bp to close near 5.11% after touching 5.135%, and the 5-year crossed 5% for the first time since 2007.

The Fed sits at 3.75–4.00% after a 12–0 vote on 16 September; the next meeting is 27–28 October, now 70% priced for another hike. Oil has stopped being the driver. Brent fell from about $109 on 14 September to $100.34 on the 21st and near $98 on the 22nd; reports for Wednesday conflict, ranging from below $99 to above $103, so no single figure is given here. Iran has offered to reopen Hormuz within seven days if Washington moves on its port blockade.

What was left out, and why

One name was in this list two days ago and is not any more. That reversal is explained first, because a changed mind is worth more than a consistent one.

XLE — stays outdropped 21 Sep

The 21 September edition removed XLE and set one condition for its return: the US–Iran talks collapsing. They have not. Envoys met Iranian officials for about three hours through Qatari mediators on 22 September — the first contact since the June ceasefire unravelled — and Tehran has since offered to reopen Hormuz within seven days if Washington moves on the port blockade. Rhetoric is harsh on both sides, but a channel with a concrete offer on the table is the opposite of a collapse. The re-entry trigger has not fired. There is a new argument for energy — hot PMIs mean demand-led oil strength, not just supply fear — and it was weighed. It was rejected because reinstating a position three sessions after removing it, on one survey print and a disputed oil bounce, is headline-chasing. The condition stands: talks collapse, it returns.

SMH · SOXX+82% / +113% in H1 2026

Excluded on price, not on thesis. Wednesday's Nasdaq drop of 1.13% shows the risk: long-duration growth multiples are the first thing a rising 10-year compresses, and Governor Barr has now named AI investment demand as an inflation source. Nvidia alone is near a fifth of SMH, and the bull case needs hyperscaler capex above the $1trn global data-centre projection; Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon are guiding past $400bn for 2026 against roughly $230bn in 2024. If two of the four trim, the multiple has no support. RSP and VEA carry diffuse exposure already.

GLD−4.8% YTD

Gold peaked at $5,589 in January, then fell about 14% in Q2, its worst quarter in a decade, and Goldman cut its year-end target to $4,900 from $5,400. A hiking Fed raises the carry cost of a zero-yield asset. VTIP does the defensive job and pays a coupon.

XLUUtilities index +16.2% YTD

Right story, wrong rate regime — and Wednesday proved it. Utilities were among the three sectors leading the decline as the 10-year spiked. US data-centre capacity is set to rise 197% by 2030, but utilities are bond proxies carrying heavy debt, and with the 10-year above 5% that trade is fighting the tape.

TLT · long duration10-yr at a 19-year high

The last edition called this "tempting if the policy-error thesis is right". That thesis has weakened, and the 10-year added 15bp in a single session. Long duration is the one asset class this regime is actively punishing. VTIP holds the defensive slot with about 2.4 years of duration instead of 15-plus.

USO+103.8% YTD

The year's best-performing fund and still the wrong vehicle — it owns futures, so roll mechanics drive the result. With oil prices this week disputed even between reputable sources, a futures-roll product is the last place to express a view.
