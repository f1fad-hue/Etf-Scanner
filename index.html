Why not maximum growth

  • All stocks ends about $3,114 ahead over ten years, but its 54% worst case would take about 11.8 years to recover — longer than the horizon.
  • The optimizer leans on XLV (57% of the stock side) because healthcare fell least in 2008 and 2020. That is a pattern, not a guarantee.
  • It is forecast-sensitive: with Schwab's lower 5.9% for US stocks, the same 21% budget would hold only 29% in stocks, tilted to VEA.

Assumptions

  • Returns: J.P. Morgan 2026 forecasts — US large cap 6.7% a year (RSP, XLV, ITA), developed ex-US 7.4% (VEA) — less each fund's fee. VTIP 4.0% is an assumption between Schwab's 3.3% for cash and J.P. Morgan's 5.3% for bonds.
  • Worst crash: each fund's actual fall — RSP 59.9%, VEA 60.7%, ITA 59.7%, XLV 39.2%, all bottoming 9 March 2009. VTIP (launched 2012) uses its worst, 6.3% in March 2020.
  • Every fund at least 5%, no stock fund above 30%, rebalanced to target. Recovery time assumes expected returns after a crash — past recoveries were faster, future crashes can be deeper.
  • Money needed within about a year belongs in cash or short bonds whatever the budget.

Stocks pay almost nothing over bonds

The S&P's earnings yield is about 5.2%; the 10-year pays 5.11%. That premium of roughly 0.1 point compares with a historical 3–4. Short-term money should stay in short bonds until the signals below fire.
