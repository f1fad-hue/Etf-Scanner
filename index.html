Three readings of the same tape

Trend, sentiment and volatility disagree right now. That disagreement is the whole investment case — it is what makes the five picks below look different from a 2025 portfolio.

01Market trend — strong index, thin participation

The index level flatters the market. The S&P closed at 7,650.50 on 18 September, up roughly 11.7% on price this year, and Goldman Sachs lifted its year-end target to 8,000 on 2026 EPS of about $340. But only ~33% of members sit above their 50-day average and ~53% above their 200-day.

Leadership has quietly moved offshore. Developed international is beating the S&P by a wide margin in 2026, helped by cheaper multiples and a dollar that weakened for most of the year.

Read: an uptrend carried by fewer and fewer stocks. Own the trend, but stop paying up for the narrow part of it.

02Broad sentiment — defensive at the highs

CNN's Fear & Greed Index printed 29 on 18 September — "Fear" — with the index within a few percent of its record. The S&P Global Investment Manager Index fell 19 points for September, a sharp drop in stated risk appetite.

Flows corroborate the anxiety. Gold ETFs absorbed roughly $2bn in the five sessions before the Fed meeting, after a record-adjacent $18bn month in August that lifted global gold ETF assets 16% to $615bn.

Read: nobody trusts this tape. Positioning that defensive near a high argues for broadening, not for a top — but it also means the crowd is paying up for protection.

03Volatility — cheap in stocks, expensive in bonds

Equity vol has drained away: the VIX closed 14.81 on 18 September, down from 17.71 two sessions earlier, 20.66 in late July and 21.51 in early June. It sits near its own ten-year average.

The bond market is where the stress lives. The 10-year touched 5.01% on 14 September — the highest since October 2023 — before the Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00%. Futures now price roughly 4.2% by December and 4.6% by September 2027.

What was left out, and why

The near-misses matter as much as the picks — most were excluded on price or on rate sensitivity, not on the strength of their story.

SMH · SOXX+82% / +113% in H1 2026

Excluded on price, not on thesis. Nvidia alone is near a fifth of SMH, and the bull case needs hyperscaler capex to hold above the $1trn global data-centre projection — Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon are guiding past $400bn for 2026 against roughly $230bn in 2024. If two of the four trim, the multiple has no support. RSP and VEA already carry diffuse exposure.

GLD−4.8% YTD

Gold peaked at $5,589 in January, then fell about 14% in Q2 — its worst quarter in a decade — and Goldman cut its year-end target to $4,900 from $5,400. A hiking Fed raises the carry cost of a zero-yield asset. VTIP does the same defensive job and pays a coupon.

XLU+16.2% YTD

Right story, wrong rate regime. US data-centre capacity is set to rise 197% by 2030, but utilities are bond proxies and the 10-year is near 4.9%. The AI-power trade works better through the companies building the capacity than through the regulated operators financing it.

TLT · long durationHiking cycle

The Fed has just started raising, with another ~85bp priced by September 2027. There is no reason to own the long end into that.

USO+103.8% YTD

The single best-performing fund of 2026, and still the wrong vehicle. USO owns futures, so roll mechanics — not oil companies — drive the result. XLE expresses the same macro with free cash flow and a ~3.1% dividend behind it.
