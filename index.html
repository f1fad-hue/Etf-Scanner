The regime, in one line

The Fed is tightening into an energy shock that is already deflating. Core CPI is 2.4% and easing. The entire gap to the 3.4% headline is oil — and Brent has fallen from $109 on 14 September to $100.34, with Hormuz flows back to roughly 80% of pre-war levels. If that holds, headline converges down toward core and the September hike lands into disinflation. If it breaks — tankers are still being hit and the Houthis struck Riyadh on Saturday — the opposite. The five below are built so that neither outcome is fatal.

Three readings of the same tape

Trend, sentiment and volatility still disagree — and after Monday's record close in the Nasdaq against a Fear & Greed reading of 29, they disagree more sharply than a week ago.

01Market trend — records on thin participation

The S&P rose 1.49% on Monday to 7,764.70, its best day since early August, and the Nasdaq closed at a record on AI strength. Through Friday the S&P was up 11.7% on price for the year; Goldman Sachs targets 8,000 on 2026 EPS near $340.

Participation has not kept up. Roughly 33% of members sit above their 50-day average and 53% above their 200-day, while the Magnificent Seven are 33.9% of index weight after peaking at a record 35% in June. One number complicates the simple story: equal weight is not lagging. RSP returned 15.46% through 31 August, ahead of the cap-weighted index.

02Broad sentiment — fear at a record high

CNN's Fear & Greed Index read 29 on 21 September — "Fear" — on the same day the Nasdaq set a record close. That is an unusual pairing, and it is the second week running that sentiment has refused to follow price.

Positioning agrees with the mood, not the tape. The S&P Global Investment Manager Index fell 19 points for September, and gold ETFs took in $18bn in August, the second-largest month on record, lifting global gold ETF assets 16% to $615bn.

03Volatility — the stress moved from oil to policy

Equity volatility is subdued: the VIX is near 14.8, having ranged no higher than 18.94 over the past month. Rate volatility is where the action was — the 10-year touched 5.014% on 14 September, its highest since October 2023, before easing to 4.96%.

On 16 September the Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75–4.00% in a 12–0 vote, its first hike since 2023. Chair Warsh's press conference moved October hike odds to 49% and the odds of at least one more this year to 87%. The next meeting is 27–28 October. Meanwhile Brent lost 3.4% on Monday to $100.34, a fourth consecutive decline, after Washington signalled openness to meeting Iran's president at the UN this week.

What was left out, and why

One name was in this list two days ago and is not any more. That reversal is explained first, because a changed mind is worth more than a consistent one.

XLE — dropped from #5+49.5% YTD to 15 Sep

The 19 September edition held XLE as an inflation and geopolitical hedge, scored 2/5 at twelve months and ten years, with an explicit note: hold it for a reason, and sell it when the reason ends. The reason is ending. Brent has fallen four sessions straight to $100.34, Hormuz flows are back to roughly 80% of pre-war levels, and Washington has signalled it may meet Iran's president at the UN this week. Energy equities sell off hard on exactly these signals. Add that consensus already sees sector earnings contracting about 5% in 2027 after a 57% surge in 2026, and a position up nearly 50% on the year is an expensive hedge against a risk that is visibly receding. Following the earlier rule rather than defending the earlier pick, it is out. It goes back in if the talks collapse.

SMH · SOXX+82% / +113% in H1 2026

Excluded on price, not on thesis, and Monday's AI-led record makes that harder to hold — but not wrong. Nvidia alone is near a fifth of SMH, and the bull case needs hyperscaler capex above the $1trn global data-centre projection; Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon are guiding past $400bn for 2026 against roughly $230bn in 2024. If two of the four trim, the multiple has no support. RSP and VEA carry diffuse exposure already.

GLD−4.8% YTD

Gold peaked at $5,589 in January, then fell about 14% in Q2, its worst quarter in a decade, and Goldman cut its year-end target to $4,900 from $5,400. A hiking Fed raises the carry cost of a zero-yield asset. VTIP does the defensive job and pays a coupon.

XLUUtilities index +16.2% YTD

Right story, wrong rate regime. US data-centre capacity is set to rise 197% by 2030, but utilities are bond proxies carrying heavy debt, and the 10-year is still near 4.96% with the Fed signalling more. Healthcare offers a defensive sleeve without the rate sensitivity.

TLT · long duration87% odds of another hike

Tempting if the policy-error thesis is right, and that is exactly why it is dangerous: it pays only on that one outcome, and the market still prices 87% odds of another hike this year. VTIP takes the same direction with a fraction of the duration.

USO+103.8% YTD

The year's best-performing fund and still the wrong vehicle — it owns futures, so roll mechanics drive the result. With oil now falling, that mechanic works against the holder twice over.
